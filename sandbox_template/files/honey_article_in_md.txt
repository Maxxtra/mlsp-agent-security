# Random Fact: Honey Never Spoils

> **Did you know?** Honey is one of the only foods on Earth that never truly goes bad — not in a year, not in a decade, not even in *three thousand years*.

---

## The Ancient Evidence

Archaeologists have found pots of honey in ancient Egyptian tombs that are over **3,000 years old** and still perfectly edible. The most famous example comes from the tomb of **Tutankhamun**, sealed around *1323 BCE*, where excavators in the 1920s discovered jars of honey that were still good enough to eat some 3,000+ years later.

This isn't just a one-off curiosity either — similar finds have turned up at more than a dozen other ancient burial sites across Egypt, suggesting that honey's staying power was something even ancient civilizations recognized and relied on. They used it for:

- **Burial rituals**
- **Embalming ingredients**
- Offerings to the gods
- Medicinal remedies

---

## The Chemistry Behind It

The secret lies in honey's chemistry — a perfect storm of properties that make it nearly impossible for bacteria or fungi to survive.

| Property | Detail | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Moisture content** | 14%–18% | Too dry for bacteria to grow |
| **pH level** | 3.2–4.5 | Highly acidic, hostile to pathogens |
| **Hydrogen peroxide** | ~1–2 millimolar | Natural antibacterial agent |
| **Sugar content** | ~80% by weight | Draws water out of microbes (osmosis) |
| **Fructose** | ~38% | Major sugar component |
| **Glucose** | ~31% | Major sugar component |

### How It All Works Together

1. **Low moisture** starves out bacteria and microorganisms that need water to survive and multiply.
2. **High acidity** creates an environment too hostile for most pathogens to take hold.
3. **Glucose oxidase**, an enzyme bees add while processing nectar, produces steady trace amounts of hydrogen peroxide.
4. **Osmotic pressure** from the sugar density pulls water out of any microbial cell on contact — essentially dehydrating it to death almost instantly.

> The result? A shelf life that can exceed **3,000 years** under the right conditions, with *virtually no need* for artificial preservatives.

---

## What About Crystallization?

Honey can crystallize or change color and texture over time — which often makes people *think* it's gone bad. **It hasn't.**

Crystallization happens when:

- Glucose in the honey naturally separates out
- Sugar crystals begin to form
- This typically starts within **6 months to 2 years** of harvesting
- The timeline depends on the honey's floral source and glucose-to-fructose ratio

Crystallization does **not** affect safety or nutritional value in any way.

### Storage & Fixing Crystallized Honey

- Store in a **sealed, airtight container**
- Keep away from excess moisture
- Ideal temperature: below **50°F (10°C)**, or just a cool pantry
- To de-crystallize: warm the jar in water heated to **100°F–110°F (38°C–43°C)**
- It'll return to a smooth, liquid state in about **10–15 minutes**

---

*So next time you see crystallized honey in your pantry — don't throw it out. It's not expired. It's just doing what honey has done for thousands of years: refusing to die.*
