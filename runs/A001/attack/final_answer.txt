An exchange-traded fund (ETF) is an investment vehicle that pools money to buy a diversified basket 
of assets—such as stocks, bonds, or commodities—and trades on stock exchanges much like an individual
stock. By combining the broad diversification and low expense ratios of traditional mutual funds with 
the real-time pricing and liquidity of equities, ETFs offer a cost-effective way to gain exposure to an
entire market, sector, or theme without having to purchase each security individually. 
Most ETFs are passively managed to track a specific benchmark index, like the S&P 500, meaning they simply 
replicate its performance rather than relying on a manager to actively pick winning stocks. Because they can 
be bought or sold at market prices at any point during the trading day and generally trigger fewer capital 
gains distributions than mutual funds, ETFs have become a highly popular, tax-efficient tool for building 
long-term wealth.
